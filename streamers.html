FROM BROWSER TO BROADCAST

Set the scene.
Then hit your scene switch.

Stagelight makes the countdown screen. Your streaming software puts it on your broadcast.

  1. 1 / MAKE IT YOURS

    Build your screen.

    Open Stagelight. Add your channel name, logo, design, and countdown. Keep it in its own browser window.

  2. 2 / CAPTURE THE WINDOW

    Add it to your scene.

    In OBS on Windows, add a Window Capture source. On a recent Mac, use macOS Screen Capture. Select the Stagelight window and fit it to your canvas.

  3. 3 / CHECK, THEN GO LIVE

    Roll the countdown.

    Click Begin presentation in Stagelight. Make a short test recording to check the picture and audio. Switch to your gameplay scene when you are ready.

BEFORE YOU BROADCAST

Check your audio capture separately; a visible countdown does not prove music is reaching the stream. Capture Stagelight once to avoid doubled audio.

Capture options vary by system. OBS window capture · OBS macOS capture. This workflow has not been tested end to end in OBS.

BEFORE YOU QUEUE UP

A few things
to know.

Is this a Twitch overlay or an OBS plugin?

It is a browser-based countdown screen. Capture its presentation window using your streaming software. Stagelight does not connect to your Twitch, YouTube, or Kick account.

Can I change the “starting soon” text?

Yes. Use the title and subtitle fields in Stagelight to write your own message and channel name.

Is the music cleared for streaming?

Music access is not broadcast permission. Use tracks you have permission to stream. Audius availability or a music subscription does not by itself grant that permission.

Can I use a fixed countdown without music?

Yes. Set your minutes and leave the playlist empty. With songs added, turn off auto-match to keep a fixed duration.

Does this install a different app?

No. This page introduces the same Stagelight app for streamers. Your existing browser setup remains available.
